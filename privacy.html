Privacy Policy: Everest Chess 3D

Effective date: October 28, 2026
Developer: Elias Utegaard (an individual developer)
Contact: support.utegaardgames@gmail.com

This policy explains what information the Everest Chess 3D app uses, where it is kept, who can see it, and the choices you have. "We", "us" and "our" mean Elias Utegaard, the individual developer of the app.

1. The short version

  • Everest Chess 3D has no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party SDKs, and we don't run any servers or accounts of our own.
  • Lessons, games against the computer, and two-player games on one device work without an internet connection and without signing in. What the app saves for these stays on your device.
  • If you choose to play a friend online, the app uses Apple's Game Center. Apple stores the moves and status of those games and passes turns between you and your friend. Your Game Center nickname is shown to your friend, and theirs to you. If you're signed in to Game Center, your scores and achievements also go to Game Center, where Apple shows them to other players according to Apple's rules (see section 3).
  • We don't sell or share your information, and we don't use it for advertising.

2. What the app saves on your device

To work properly, the app saves the following on your iPhone or iPad only:

  • Settings, such as your board and piece set (a fresh install starts with light and dark wood pieces), the app language, sounds and music, Everest's tips, whether "Read tips aloud" is on, threat hints and the turn indicator.
  • Progress, such as how many games you've won against the computer, which unlocks boards and piece sets.
  • Player names for two-player games, if you type them (up to 20 characters each). These are used only to label the two sides on the board. You can leave them blank, and then the app shows "White" and "Black".
  • Online play only: the time you last sent a nudge in each online game, so the app can limit nudges to one per game per day.
  • Temporary files the app can rebuild, such as prepared images of the pieces, in the app's cache.

The app doesn't send any of this to us. When you're signed in to Game Center, only the game information, scores and achievements described in section 3 go to Apple's Game Center; the other items above stay on your device.

3. Online play with friends (Game Center)

Online play is optional. It uses Game Center, a service provided and run by Apple, and it needs an internet connection and a Game Center account (signed in with your Apple Account in your device's settings).

How it works. You invite someone you choose through Game Center, using Apple's invite screen. The app turns off Game Center's automatic matching, so there is no random matchmaking with strangers. You and your friend take turns whenever you like. Game Center holds the game between turns and can notify your friend when it's their move. When it's your friend's turn, you can send a short reminder ("nudge"), at most once per game per day.

There is no chat. You can't type or record messages to other players. The only messages are fixed, pre-written ones from the app, such as "It's your move" and the nudge, shown in your friend's language.

What information is involved:

  • Your Game Center profile information that Apple provides to the app: your Game Center display name and Game Center player identifiers. The display name is normally your Game Center nickname. Apple may show your real name instead to friends you connected with before iOS 10, and Apple says children's accounts never show real names to friends. The app uses the player identifiers only on your device, to tell which player is you in a game. It never shows them on screen, and it doesn't send them to us or anyone else; Game Center uses its own identifiers to run the match.
  • Your friend's Game Center display name, shown so you know who you're playing.
  • Game information: the starting position, the list of moves, whose turn it is, the result (win, loss, draw, resignation or timeout), a turn counter and the time of the last nudge. The app sends this to Game Center at the end of each turn, and your friend's copy of the app reads it from there.
  • Turn and nudge notifications sent through Game Center. These use the app's fixed wording and normally include your Game Center display name, for example "[name] is waiting for your move." If Apple marks your account as underage, restricts personalized messages, or the app can't tell either way, the app leaves your name out.
  • Whether online play is allowed on your device. The app reads Apple's settings that say whether multiplayer games or personal messages are restricted (for example by Screen Time) or whether Apple marks the account as underage. If multiplayer games are restricted in any way (including "friends only"), the app hides online play.

How this information is used: only to run your games with friends: to show who you're playing, keep the board and turns in sync, enforce the rules, notify the player whose turn it is, and respect parental controls. We don't use it for advertising, profiling or analytics.

Who holds it: Apple stores the game information on its Game Center servers. We don't run a server and the app doesn't send this information to us. Apple's handling of Game Center data, including what Apple itself collects when you use Game Center, is covered by Apple's privacy policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) and Apple's "Game Center & Privacy" notice (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/game-center/).

Leaderboards and achievements. If you're signed in to Game Center, the app reports your results to Game Center: your scores for the app's leaderboards and the achievements you earn. Results you earn while signed out are saved on your device and sent when you later sign in (see section 8). Apple shows these in Game Center. Apple says that for games with global leaderboards, your Game Center nickname, avatar and scores are visible to all Game Center users, and that achievements and other activity follow your Game Center settings (for children's accounts, friends only). If Apple marks your account as underage, or the app can't tell, the app doesn't send your scores to leaderboards. If you don't want your results on Game Center, stay signed out of Game Center on this device; the rest of the app still works.

4. What we don't do

  • We don't show ads, and the app contains no advertising code.
  • We don't use analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools of our own or from any third party, and we don't use your device's advertising identifier.
  • We don't have our own accounts or sign-in. Online play, leaderboards and achievements use your Game Center account, which Apple provides; everything else works without signing in.
  • We don't ask for or use your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • The app doesn't include web pages inside it. Its only link out is to this privacy policy, which opens in your browser.
  • We don't sell, rent or share your personal information, and we don't use it for targeted advertising.

5. Third-party services

The only outside company involved is Apple, through Game Center, and only for online play, leaderboards and achievements. Apple runs Game Center as its own service. It receives the game information and Game Center details described in section 3 and handles them under Apple's Privacy Policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) and Apple's "Game Center & Privacy" notice (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/game-center/). We've reviewed those and believe they protect your information at least as well as this policy does. Apple, not us, decides how Apple uses Game Center data for its own purposes (for example, to run and improve Game Center), and Apple's notices explain that. The app contains no other third-party services or SDKs, and we don't share your information with anyone else.

You buy the app through Apple's App Store. Apple handles payment, and we don't receive your payment details.

App analytics from Apple. If you've turned on "Share With App Developers" (Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), Apple may give us crash reports and statistics about how the app is used. Apple says it gives developers this information only when it is "aggregated or in a form that does not personally identify you." You can turn this off in the same setting at any time.

6. The chess engine and Everest's voice

  • The computer opponent (the open-source Arasan chess engine) runs entirely on your device. Games against the computer aren't sent anywhere.
  • Everest the red panda speaks with pre-recorded, computer-generated (synthetic) audio clips built into the app. The app doesn't use your microphone and doesn't record or listen to you.
  • Read tips aloud (off by default) uses Apple's built-in speech synthesis on your device to read tips out loud.

7. How long information is kept, and how to delete it

  • On your device: settings, progress and player names stay until you change them or delete the app. Deleting the app removes them from that device. If you back up your device with iCloud Backup or to a computer, iOS may include them in that backup; Apple controls those backups.
  • Online games: Apple keeps game information on Game Center. Each turn uses Game Center's default turn timeout, which Apple documents as one week. Apple says it keeps Game Center information as long as you keep the Apple Account linked to your Game Center account. We can't see or delete data held by Apple; to manage it, use your Game Center and Apple Account settings or contact Apple (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/contact/).
  • Emails to us: we keep support emails only as long as we need them to help you, and no longer than 12 months, then delete them. See sections 9 and 10.

8. Your choices

  • Don't use online play. Everything except online games, leaderboards and achievements works without Game Center.
  • Stop online play at any time. You can resign any online game in the app, and you can sign out of Game Center in your device's settings.
  • Playing while signed out. If you play while signed out of Game Center, progress you earn (for example wins and achievements) is stored on your device and sent to Game Center after you later sign in.
  • Parental controls: parents can limit or block Game Center features, such as multiplayer games and connecting with friends, under Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions. The app checks these settings: it hides online play when multiplayer games are restricted, and it leaves names out of notifications when personalized messages are restricted.
  • Delete local data by deleting the app.
  • Ask us. Email us to ask what information we hold about you or to ask us to delete it. Apart from emails you send us, we don't hold any information about you on our own systems.

9. Children

Everest Chess 3D is rated 4+, so people of any age, including children, may play it. It isn't in the App Store's Kids category. Here is how the app handles children's information.

  • We don't collect personal information from children. The app never asks anyone for their age, name, email address, phone number or other contact details. It has no chat, no accounts of our own, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. We don't run servers, so nothing the app does sends information to us.
  • Online play uses Apple's Game Center and Apple's rules for children. A child can play online only while signed in to Game Center. Apple says children's Game Center accounts can send only preset messages, have voice chat turned off, and never show their real names to friends. Parents can block multiplayer games and adding friends in Screen Time (Settings > Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions). If multiplayer games are restricted in any way, including "friends only", the app hides online play.
  • Extra limits for children's accounts. If Apple marks a Game Center account as underage, or personalized messages are restricted, or the app can't tell, the app leaves that player's Game Center name out of turn and nudge notifications (the friend just sees "It's your move"). If the account is marked underage, or the app can't tell, the app doesn't send that player's scores to Game Center leaderboards.
  • What a friend sees. In an online game, the friend the child is playing sees the child's Game Center nickname next to the board, the same way Game Center shows it to friends anywhere else.
  • Player identifiers. The app uses Game Center player identifiers only on the device, to tell which side of the board belongs to which player. It doesn't show them, send them to us, or use them to contact, track, advertise to or build a profile of anyone.
  • Emails from children. If we learn an email came from a child under 13, we'll use it only to reply once to that message. We won't add the address to any list or share it, and we'll delete the email and our reply promptly after replying (or right away if no reply is needed).
  • Parents. If you think your child has sent us personal information, email support.utegaardgames@gmail.com and we'll delete it. To manage your child's Game Center account and the data Apple holds, use Family Sharing and Screen Time, or contact Apple (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/contact/).

10. If you email us

If you email us at support.utegaardgames@gmail.com, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and we use it only to reply to you. We don't add you to a mailing list or share your email. Our email is provided by Google (Gmail). We delete support conversations once we no longer need them to help you (see section 7), or sooner if you ask. If an email comes from a child under 13, see section 9.

11. Your privacy rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us whether we have personal information about you, ask for a copy, and ask us to correct or delete it. Privacy laws in some places, including California and other U.S. states, Brazil (the LGPD) and Mexico, give people these rights, and we offer them to everyone. In practice, the only personal information we hold is emails you send us; data about your Game Center use is held by Apple. We don't sell personal information, share it for targeted advertising, or use it to profile you. To make a request, email support.utegaardgames@gmail.com. We'll respond within 15 days, and we won't treat you differently for asking. If you're in Brazil, you can also complain to Brazil's data protection authority, the ANPD. For data Apple holds about your Game Center use, contact Apple (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/contact/).

12. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll post the new version at this same web address and update the effective date above. If a change affects what data the app uses, we'll update this policy before an app version with that change is released. The app doesn't track you across other companies' apps or websites, and no other company collects information about you through the app for that purpose, so the app doesn't respond differently to "Do Not Track" signals. If we make a material change, we'll also mention it in the app's release notes.

13. Contact us

Questions about this policy or your privacy? Email support.utegaardgames@gmail.com.

Política de privacidad: Everest Chess 3D

Fecha de entrada en vigor: 28 de octubre de 2026
Desarrollador: Elias Utegaard (desarrollador independiente)
Contacto: support.utegaardgames@gmail.com

Esta política explica qué información usa la app Everest Chess 3D, dónde se guarda, quién puede verla y qué opciones tienes. "Nosotros" significa Elias Utegaard, el desarrollador independiente de la app.

1. En resumen

  • Everest Chess 3D no tiene anuncios, ni analíticas, ni rastreo, ni SDK de terceros, y no operamos servidores ni cuentas propias.
  • Las lecciones, las partidas contra la computadora y las partidas de dos jugadores en un mismo dispositivo funcionan sin conexión a internet y sin iniciar sesión. Lo que la app guarda para esto se queda en tu dispositivo.
  • Si decides jugar en línea con un amigo, la app usa Game Center de Apple. Apple guarda las jugadas y el estado de esas partidas y pasa los turnos entre tú y tu amigo. Tu amigo ve tu apodo de Game Center y tú ves el suyo. Si iniciaste sesión en Game Center, tus puntuaciones y logros también se envían a Game Center, donde Apple los muestra a otros jugadores según sus propias reglas (ver la sección 3).
  • No vendemos ni compartimos tu información y no la usamos para publicidad.

2. Lo que la app guarda en tu dispositivo

Para funcionar, la app guarda lo siguiente solo en tu iPhone o iPad:

  • Ajustes, como el tablero y el juego de piezas (una instalación nueva empieza con piezas de madera clara y oscura), el idioma de la app, sonidos y música, los consejos de Everest, si "Leer consejos en voz alta" está activado, los avisos de amenazas y el indicador de turno.
  • Progreso, como cuántas partidas le has ganado a la computadora, lo que desbloquea tableros y juegos de piezas.
  • Nombres de los jugadores en partidas de dos jugadores, si los escribes (hasta 20 caracteres cada uno). Solo se usan para identificar los dos lados del tablero. Puedes dejarlos en blanco y la app mostrará "Blancas" y "Negras".
  • Solo para el juego en línea: la hora en que enviaste el último recordatorio en cada partida en línea, para limitar los recordatorios a uno por partida al día.
  • Archivos temporales que la app puede volver a crear, como imágenes preparadas de las piezas, en la caché de la app.

La app no nos envía nada de esto. Cuando tienes la sesión iniciada en Game Center, solo la información de las partidas, las puntuaciones y los logros descritos en la sección 3 van a Game Center de Apple; lo demás se queda en tu dispositivo.

3. Juego en línea con amigos (Game Center)

El juego en línea es opcional. Usa Game Center, un servicio que ofrece y opera Apple, y necesita conexión a internet y una cuenta de Game Center (con tu cuenta de Apple, en los ajustes de tu dispositivo).

Cómo funciona. Invitas a quien tú elijas a través de Game Center, con la pantalla de invitación de Apple. La app desactiva el emparejamiento automático de Game Center, así que no hay emparejamiento al azar con desconocidos. Tú y tu amigo juegan por turnos cuando quieran. Game Center guarda la partida entre turnos y puede avisarle a tu amigo cuando le toca. Cuando es el turno de tu amigo, puedes enviarle un recordatorio breve como máximo una vez por partida al día.

No hay chat. No puedes escribir ni grabar mensajes para otros jugadores. Los únicos mensajes son textos fijos de la app, como "Es tu turno" y el recordatorio, que se muestran en el idioma de tu amigo.

Qué información se usa:

  • La información de tu perfil de Game Center que Apple le da a la app: tu nombre visible de Game Center y los identificadores de jugador de Game Center. El nombre visible normalmente es tu apodo de Game Center. Apple puede mostrar tu nombre real a los amigos con los que te conectaste antes de iOS 10, y Apple indica que las cuentas infantiles nunca muestran el nombre real a los amigos. La app usa los identificadores de jugador solo en tu dispositivo, para saber cuál de los jugadores eres tú en una partida. Nunca los muestra en pantalla y no nos los envía ni a nosotros ni a nadie más; Game Center usa sus propios identificadores para gestionar la partida.
  • El nombre visible de tu amigo en Game Center, para que sepas contra quién juegas.
  • Información de la partida: la posición inicial, la lista de jugadas, a quién le toca, el resultado (victoria, derrota, tablas, abandono o tiempo agotado), un contador de turnos y la hora del último recordatorio. La app la envía a Game Center al terminar cada turno y la app de tu amigo la lee desde ahí.
  • Notificaciones de turno y de recordatorio enviadas mediante Game Center. Usan el texto fijo de la app y normalmente incluyen tu nombre visible de Game Center, por ejemplo: "[nombre] está esperando tu jugada". Si Apple marca tu cuenta como de un menor o restringe los mensajes personalizados, o si la app no puede saberlo, la app no incluye tu nombre.
  • Si el juego en línea está permitido en tu dispositivo. La app lee los ajustes de Apple que indican si los juegos multijugador o los mensajes personales están restringidos (por ejemplo, con Tiempo en pantalla) o si Apple marca la cuenta como de un menor. Si los juegos multijugador están restringidos de cualquier forma (incluido "solo amigos"), la app oculta el juego en línea.

Para qué se usa: solo para tus partidas con amigos: mostrar contra quién juegas, mantener el tablero y los turnos sincronizados, aplicar las reglas, avisar al jugador al que le toca y respetar los controles parentales. No la usamos para publicidad, perfiles ni analíticas.

Quién la guarda: Apple guarda la información de la partida en los servidores de Game Center. No operamos ningún servidor y la app no nos envía esta información. El manejo que hace Apple de los datos de Game Center, incluido lo que Apple recopila cuando usas Game Center, se rige por la política de privacidad de Apple (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) y su aviso "Game Center y privacidad" (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/es-419/game-center/).

Tablas de clasificación y logros. Si iniciaste sesión en Game Center, la app envía tus resultados a Game Center: tus puntuaciones para las tablas de clasificación de la app y los logros que obtengas. Los resultados que obtengas sin haber iniciado sesión se guardan en tu dispositivo y se envían cuando inicies sesión más adelante (ver la sección 8). Apple los muestra en Game Center. Apple indica que, en los juegos con tablas de clasificación globales, tu apodo, tu avatar y tus puntuaciones de Game Center son visibles para todos los usuarios de Game Center, y que los logros y demás actividad siguen tus ajustes de Game Center (en las cuentas infantiles, solo para amigos). Si Apple marca tu cuenta como de un menor, o si la app no puede saberlo, la app no envía tus puntuaciones a las tablas de clasificación. Si no quieres que tus resultados aparezcan en Game Center, no inicies sesión en Game Center en este dispositivo; el resto de la app sigue funcionando.

Política de Privacidade: Everest Chess 3D

Data de vigência: 28 de outubro de 2026
Desenvolvedor: Elias Utegaard (desenvolvedor individual)
Contato: support.utegaardgames@gmail.com

Esta política explica quais informações o app Everest Chess 3D usa, onde elas ficam guardadas, quem pode vê-las e quais escolhas você tem. "Nós" significa Elias Utegaard, o desenvolvedor individual do app.

1. Resumo

  • O Everest Chess 3D não tem anúncios, análises (analytics), rastreamento nem SDKs de terceiros, e não mantemos servidores nem contas próprias.
  • As lições, as partidas contra o computador e as partidas para dois jogadores no mesmo aparelho funcionam sem internet e sem login. O que o app salva para isso fica no seu aparelho.
  • Se você escolher jogar online com um amigo, o app usa o Game Center da Apple. A Apple guarda os lances e a situação dessas partidas e passa a vez entre você e seu amigo. Seu amigo vê o seu apelido do Game Center, e você vê o dele. Se você estiver conectado ao Game Center, suas pontuações e conquistas também vão para o Game Center, onde a Apple as mostra a outros jogadores de acordo com as regras da Apple (veja a seção 3).
  • Não vendemos nem compartilhamos suas informações e não as usamos para publicidade.

2. O que o app salva no seu aparelho

Para funcionar, o app salva o seguinte apenas no seu iPhone ou iPad:

  • Ajustes, como o tabuleiro e o conjunto de peças (uma instalação nova começa com peças de madeira clara e escura), o idioma do app, sons e música, as dicas do Everest, se "Ler dicas em voz alta" está ativado, os avisos de ameaça e o indicador de vez.
  • Progresso, como quantas partidas você venceu contra o computador, o que desbloqueia tabuleiros e conjuntos de peças.
  • Nomes dos jogadores em partidas para dois, se você digitá-los (até 20 caracteres cada). Eles servem só para identificar os dois lados do tabuleiro. Você pode deixá-los em branco, e o app mostra "Brancas" e "Pretas".
  • Só para o jogo online: o horário do último lembrete que você enviou em cada partida online, para limitar os lembretes a um por partida por dia.
  • Arquivos temporários que o app pode recriar, como imagens prontas das peças, no cache do app.

O app não envia nada disso para nós. Quando você está conectado ao Game Center, só as informações da partida, as pontuações e as conquistas descritas na seção 3 vão para o Game Center da Apple; o resto fica no seu aparelho.

3. Jogo online com amigos (Game Center)

O jogo online é opcional. Ele usa o Game Center, um serviço oferecido e operado pela Apple, e precisa de internet e de uma conta do Game Center (com a sua Conta Apple, nos ajustes do aparelho).

Como funciona. Você convida quem você escolher pelo Game Center, usando a tela de convite da Apple. O app desativa as partidas automáticas do Game Center, então não há partidas aleatórias com desconhecidos. Vocês jogam por turnos quando quiserem. O Game Center guarda a partida entre as jogadas e pode avisar seu amigo quando for a vez dele. Quando for a vez do seu amigo, você pode enviar um lembrete curto, no máximo uma vez por partida por dia.

Não há chat. Você não pode digitar nem gravar mensagens para outros jogadores. As únicas mensagens são textos fixos do app, como "Sua vez" e o lembrete, mostrados no idioma do seu amigo.

Quais informações são usadas:

  • As informações do seu perfil do Game Center que a Apple fornece ao app: seu nome de exibição no Game Center e identificadores de jogador do Game Center. O nome de exibição normalmente é o seu apelido do Game Center. A Apple pode mostrar o seu nome verdadeiro a amigos com quem você se conectou antes do iOS 10, e a Apple diz que contas infantis nunca mostram o nome verdadeiro aos amigos. O app usa os identificadores de jogador só no seu aparelho, para saber qual jogador é você em uma partida. Ele nunca os mostra na tela e não os envia para nós nem para mais ninguém; o Game Center usa os próprios identificadores para gerenciar a partida.
  • O nome de exibição do seu amigo no Game Center, para você saber com quem está jogando.
  • Informações da partida: a posição inicial, a lista de lances, de quem é a vez, o resultado (vitória, derrota, empate, desistência ou tempo esgotado), um contador de jogadas e o horário do último lembrete. O app envia isso ao Game Center ao fim de cada jogada, e o app do seu amigo lê de lá.
  • Notificações de vez e de lembrete enviadas pelo Game Center. Elas usam o texto fixo do app e normalmente incluem o seu nome de exibição no Game Center, por exemplo: "[nome] está esperando o seu lance". Se a Apple marcar a sua conta como de menor de idade ou restringir mensagens personalizadas, ou se o app não conseguir saber, o app não inclui o seu nome.
  • Se o jogo online é permitido no seu aparelho. O app lê os ajustes da Apple que indicam se jogos multijogador ou mensagens pessoais estão restritos (por exemplo, pelo Tempo de Uso) ou se a Apple marca a conta como de menor de idade. Se os jogos multijogador estiverem restritos de qualquer forma (inclusive "só amigos"), o app esconde o jogo online.

Para que são usadas: só para as suas partidas com amigos: mostrar com quem você está jogando, manter o tabuleiro e as jogadas sincronizados, aplicar as regras, avisar quem tem a vez e respeitar o controle parental. Não as usamos para publicidade, criação de perfis nem análises.

Quem guarda: a Apple guarda as informações da partida nos servidores do Game Center. Não temos servidor, e o app não envia essas informações para nós. O tratamento dos dados do Game Center pela Apple, incluindo o que a própria Apple coleta quando você usa o Game Center, segue a política de privacidade da Apple (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) e o aviso "Game Center e Privacidade" da Apple (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/pt-br/game-center/).

Placares e conquistas. Se você estiver conectado ao Game Center, o app envia seus resultados ao Game Center: suas pontuações nos placares do app e as conquistas que você obtiver. Os resultados que você obtiver enquanto estiver desconectado ficam guardados no seu aparelho e são enviados quando você entrar no Game Center depois (veja a seção 8). A Apple os mostra no Game Center. A Apple diz que, em jogos com placares globais, o seu apelido, o seu avatar e as suas pontuações do Game Center ficam visíveis para todos os usuários do Game Center, e que as conquistas e outras atividades seguem os seus ajustes do Game Center (em contas infantis, só para amigos). Se a Apple marcar a sua conta como de menor de idade, ou se o app não conseguir saber, o app não envia as suas pontuações aos placares. Se você não quiser seus resultados no Game Center, não entre no Game Center neste aparelho; o resto do app continua funcionando.
